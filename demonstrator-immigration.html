Migration settings: what they do to jobs, growth and international education.

Three parties have put migration settings before voters: the Labor package announced on 17 September 2026, the Coalition's rule tying migration to housing completions, and One Nation's package. This is a live model of what each does over the next three federal terms, to May 2034.

A working demonstrator you can drive.

A complete system-dynamics model of net overseas migration, the labour market, growth and the international education sector - live in your browser, nothing to install. It starts from the model's state in October 2026 and runs to the end of May 2034, with a dash-dot line at the end of each federal term. Before it was allowed to look forward, the model was calibrated to 105 published series from 2018 to 2026 and checked against that record; the evidence is in the Narrative Report below. Thirty-one levers sit under headings, each party's package first. Fifteen presets set a whole configuration in one click, and two scenarios run side by side across twenty-five numbered charts. Start with a preset, then move one lever at a time.

How to read it. The model takes no side: every party's settings run through the same calibrated system, and the charts report what follows. Read shapes, turning points and the gap between two scenarios, not the third decimal place. The behavioural relationships are the main uncertainty, and the most consequential of them - how quickly new residents' spending becomes jobs, productivity growth, underlying visa demand - are exposed as levers rather than buried in the code. A forward run is a scenario, not a forecast. Where a number matters to a decision, treat it as directional.
